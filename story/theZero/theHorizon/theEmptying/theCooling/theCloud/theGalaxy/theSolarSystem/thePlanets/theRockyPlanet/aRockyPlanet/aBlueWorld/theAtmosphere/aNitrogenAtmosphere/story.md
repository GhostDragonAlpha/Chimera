**In plain words —** The air that actually formed over this world: 0.52 bar of nitrogen air that
weighs half what Earth's does, holds a deeper blue at noon, burns red at the terminator for
twenty-four minutes, keeps its clouds within the first kilometre — and would not keep a human
breathing.

*An instance of `theAtmosphere`, named by the class its own molecule puts it in.*

## The molecule nobody was told about

The law handed down a scale height of 11,312 m. Turned inside out, that number says what the
average molecule *weighs*: `μ = kT/Hg` = **29.0 g/mol**. Nobody told this chapter the air is
nitrogen — the scale height knew, and the name of this membrane is that fact. From μ alone, one
input, come four things fitted to nothing:

| derived from μ | here | Earth's air |
|---|---|---|
| gas constant | 286.7 J/kg/K | 287 |
| specific heat c_p | 1,003 J/kg/K | 1,005 |
| sound speed | 335 m/s | 343 |
| moist lapse rate | 4.7 K/km | 6.5 |

The lapse rate deserves a pause: dry air cools at `g/c_p`, and falling water gives some heat back,
so moist air cools at about two-thirds of that — 4.7 K/km here. TheTerrain's snow line was computed
from the same law; here it is the air's own number, at home.

## The sky's colour is one number

Rayleigh scattering does it all: short light scatters most (`λ⁻⁴`), and more air scatters more. The
whole sky hangs on the optical depth, and this world's column (0.71 of Earth's, from its pressure
and gravity) sets it at **0.070** mid-visible against Earth's 0.098. Then:

- **Noon**: the blue end is scattered 2.0× more than the red, so the dome is **blue** — deeper and
  slightly darker than Earth's, because there is less air to scatter with.
- **Sunset**: the path through the air stretches ~38×, the blue is scattered *away*, and what gets
  through is **red-orange**. The same equation, pointed at the horizon.
- Twilight lasts **24 minutes** — the sun drops 6° at this world's spin rate.

## Where the clouds sit

Air lifted from the surface cools at the lapse rate; the water falls out at the dewpoint. With the
surface at 6 °C and 77% humidity, the dewpoint is 2.3 °C, so the cloud deck begins at **800 m** and
weather of every kind stops at the tropopause — **21.5 km up, 179 K** — in the same pressure band
Earth's own tropopause sits in.

The rain that falls is Earth's hydrology dialled down by the cold: about **1.7 mm a day** where
Earth averages 2.7.

## Where the air ends

A molecule at the top leaves when it can go a scale height without one more collision:
`N σ H ≈ 1` puts the exobase at **278 km**. To leave for good it needs **8.6 km/s** — Earth's is
11.2, and that difference is written all the way back into which gases this world kept at all.

## The honest findings, said plainly

**This air is not breathable.** The total pressure is 0.52 bar, and at an Earth-like 21% oxygen the
partial pressure is **0.109 bar** — under the 0.16 bar line where a human hypoxiates. The oxygen
share is a *free dial* (a fact of this world's history, not of physics), and raising it past 0.31
makes the air breathable. But no number was bent to make the answer comfortable.

Two numbers are the weak ones, and the weakness is named: the tropopause's pressure band is read
from Earth's (0.1–0.22, we use 0.15), and the storm count is a scaling, not a solved circulation.

## The free dials

`o2_fraction` (0.21) · `relative_humidity` (0.77) · `cloud_cover` (0.5) — three facts about this
world's history that no law can force. Turn the oxygen up and the air becomes breathable; turn the
humidity and the cloud deck thickens and climbs. Everything else above is forced by the parents.
