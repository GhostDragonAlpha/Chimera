**In plain words —** A stepped hole in the steppe: benches spiralling down five hundred metres
to iron-red ore, because a slope you cannot stand on is a slope you cannot cut — and the first
metal this world pulls out of the ground is iron.

*An instance of `theMining`, named by the method its stripping ratio computes.*

## The ore body

Banded-iron class: the crust's 5.6% iron enriched 5.4× to a **30% ore**, near surface, with
copper at porphyry grade (0.6%) beside it. The waste is the gangue: 70% of every tonne.

## The economics, in energy

Dig and haul **2.5 kWh a tonne**; crush and grind **16.5 kWh a tonne** — Bond's law, and the
grinders are the bill, as always. Smelt and refine at iron's measured **1,450 + 400 kWh** per
tonne of metal. Against the chain, the 30% ore clears the 13% energy cutoff with room, and the
recovery is 85%.

## The method, computed

Blanket ore near the surface strips at **2.4:1 waste to ore** — well under the 8:1 pit limit —
so the first mine is a **terrace**, and the folder's name is that answer. The pit benches down
to 500 m; the geothermal limit would have allowed 1.7 km before the rock hit 45 °C.

## What it leaves

Tailings at 1× the ore tonnage, slag at 15%, **1.9 tonnes of CO₂ per tonne of steel** (measured
band) — and no subsidence: a terrace does not cave anything in. Rehabilitation at 5% of
revenue.

## The honest anchors, said plainly

The ore class (banded iron), the energy bands, the stripping ratio, the pit depth and the
inventory years are measured industry bands or scalings, named as such. The crust abundances
and the gradient arrive derived through theInterior and are never recomputed.

## The free dials

None. The geology and the energy decided all of it.

*Contained in `theMining`. What it hands on: iron and copper at stated grades and energies,
the pit's geometry, and the waste ledger — everything a smelter, a train, or a builder needs.*
