# theSolarSystem

**In plain words —** A spinning cloud cannot fall straight in. It falls freely along its axis and gets held out sideways, so it flattens into a disc with almost everything piled in the middle.

*Chapter 6.* — **the setting you fly**

A cloud does not fall straight in. It is turning, however slightly, and turning is the one thing
gravity cannot take away: **angular momentum is conserved**, so as the cloud shrinks it spins faster,
`v r = constant`. Squeeze it by a hundred and it spins a hundred times harder.

Along the axis there is nothing to resist the fall, so the cloud collapses freely there. Across the
axis the spin holds it out. So the same collapse that makes a ball on one axis makes a **disk** on
the other — and a system is born flattened, without anyone flattening it.

Almost everything reaches the centre: **the star takes ~99.9% of the mass.** But the leftover cannot
follow, because it carries nearly all the *angular momentum*, and that is what has to go somewhere.
It stays out, circling.

What circles obeys one law, and it is not imposed — it is what a central force does:

```
T² ∝ a³             Kepler's third
```

**PROVEN, not asserted:** `Chimera/core/trainables/bigbang.py` grows systems from a cloud with
nothing but softened gravity, inelastic mergers and drag — and Kepler's law is then *measured back
out* of the grown orbits' own winding periods: **slope 1.50, r² = 1.000.** Never coded in.

This membrane owns what everything inside it **shares** — the total mass, the split between centre
and disk, and the luminosity that lights everything outward. That is what a chapter is: the
description of everything contained in one membrane.

## What is not here: `theSpace`

There was a `theSpace` membrane. There is not one now, and the reason is a rule.

**Space is not a thing you can see — it is the distance between things.** That is an equation, not
matter, and a membrane has to EMIT. Having nothing to emit, it drew the interstellar medium's five
protons per cubic centimetre as visible grains: a body invented because the render wanted something
in the frame. Exactly the star-marker moon, one level up.

Every number it held was a **relation** — how far apart, how fast a circle costs, how long light
takes to cross — and a relation between two things belongs to whatever CONTAINS them. They are
derived here now, where both ends of every one of them already are.
