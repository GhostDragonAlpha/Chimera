# theSpace

**In plain words —** Empty of stuff, completely full of pull. Nothing out there slows you down, and everything out there is tugging on you.

**the medium you fly — the dark, gravity, scale**

Space is not empty. It is empty of **matter** and completely full of **gravity**, and those two facts
together are the whole character of flying out here.

## The dark

Between the worlds there are about five protons in a cubic centimetre — a density of ~10⁻²⁰ kg/m³,
which is a hundred million million million times thinner than the air you are used to. Nothing
there can push back on you. So the first law finally becomes visible: **you coast.** Cut the engine
in air and you stop; cut it here and you continue, forever, exactly as you were.

And the sky is black not because there is nothing in it but because the universe has an **age**:
light from far enough away has not arrived yet, and never will. Darkness is a statement about time.

## The pull

Gravity has no threshold and no edge. `F = GMm/r²` falls off but never reaches zero, so every mass
in the system is pulling on you right now, from any distance, and there is no shielding from it.
That is what makes space a *medium* rather than a void: **it transmits exactly one thing, and that
thing never switches off.**

So you do not steer here, you **fall** — and flying is choosing which fall you are on. To leave
anything you must climb out of its well:

```
v_esc = √(2GM/r)
```

## The scale

Nothing crosses this faster than light, so distance out here is honestly measured as **time**:

```
1 AU  =  499 s of light   (8 min 19 s)
```

The Sun you see is always eight minutes old. That is not a limitation of your instruments — it is
what "far" means.

must climb, and the light-time that turns distance into waiting.*
