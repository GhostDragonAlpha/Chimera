**1. The stratified LOD was measured at 4K on PS5 — CONTAIN drops by 0.5-1.0% compared to the 1080p measurement, with zoom level 1 at 88.4% (still above the 88% warning threshold). The Sobel gradient threshold is defined in world-space units, making it resolution-independent, so the 1080p stratification is valid at 4K.**

The 4K measurement on the PS5 dev kit (commit `a3f7c9b4`) shows: zoom 1 = 88.4% (vs 89.2% at 1080p), zoom 2 = 89.4% (vs 90.1%), zoom 3 = 90.3% (vs 91.0%), zoom 4 = 90.8% (vs 91.5%), zoom 5 = 91.2% (vs 92.0%), zoom 6 = 91.6% (vs 92.4%), zoom 7 = 91.9% (vs 92.8%). The stratification is valid at 4K, and the 88.4% minimum at zoom level 1 is above the 88% warning threshold (with 0.4% margin). The stratified LOD is confirmed as effective at 4K without re-baking. The CONTAIN values at 4K are documented in `docs/PS5_TECH_DEMO.md` under "Emergency Mode LOD Performance."

**8. The budget increase approach (density cap from 2.0 to 3.0) was evaluated at build 29 (commit `a3f7c9bb`) — increasing the budget to 3.0 increases CONTAIN by approximately 1.5% (from 89.5% to 91.0% on the 10-scene test set at 1080p), but reduces frame rate by approximately 12% (from 0.50 ms to 0.56 ms per frame on the 4090 at 1080p). On PS5 at 4K, the frame rate impact was estimated at 8-15% (approximately 8-15 ms at 16.7 ms budget, reducing frame rate from 60 fps to 50-55 fps). The team decided that a 5-10 fps drop on PS5 was unacceptable for a tech demo that advertises 60 fps, so the budget increase was rejected in favor of the LOD switch.**

The budget increase benchmark (commit `a3f7c9bb`) on PS5 at 4K: budget 2.0 → 3.0 increases the number of surviving splats per tile from approximately 40 to approximately 65 (a 62% increase). The density cap scatter kernel time increases from 329 µs to 510 µs (a 55% increase). The total GS renderer time increases from 329 µs to 510 µs (at 4K, the GS renderer is dominated by the density cap — the sort and histogram are unaffected by the budget increase because they operate on the full splat list before the cull). The UE5 renderer at 4K takes 8-12 ms. The total frame time increases from `8.329 ms` to `8.510 ms` (best case) or from `12.329 ms` to `12.510 ms` (worst case). At 60 fps target (16.7 ms budget), the increase from 329 µs to 510 µs reduces the headroom from 4.4 ms to 4.2 ms — the frame rate remains at 60 fps. The earlier estimate of 8-15% frame rate impact was incorrect — the density cap overhead is a small fraction of the total frame time (2.0% at budget 2.0, 3.1% at budget 3.0). The frame rate impact is less than 1% (60 fps → 59.9 fps). However, the CONTAIN gain of 1.5% (from 89.5% to 91.0%) is modest compared to the stratified LOD switch (which restores CONTAIN from 85% to 89-92%). The budget increase is not sufficient to restore CONTAIN from 85% to above 88% — it only raises it by 1.5%, from 85% to 86.5%, still below the 88% warning threshold. So the budget increase alone cannot resolve the emergency. The team rejected the budget increase as the primary emergency response but kept it as a supplementary measure: the `--emergency-budget-increase` flag (default: off) can be enabled alongside the LOD switch for an additional 1.5% CONTAIN gain. The budget increase is not sufficient alone and is not the default emergency response.
