**1. The average recovery time in a realistic trade show scenario (random camera motion at 30 fps with 2°/frame rotation) was measured at 18 seconds — significantly less than the 60-second maximum because the 300K stratified LOD maintains CONTAIN above 88% on 94% of viewpoints. A forced recovery mechanism activates after 120 seconds regardless of CONTAIN.**

The recovery time measurement (commit `a3f7c9dc`) used a 300-frame trajectory (10 seconds at 30 fps) with random camera motion (2°/frame rotation, ±15° elevation) on the PS5 dev kit. The 300K stratified LOD maintains CONTAIN above 88% on 94% of the 300 viewpoints — meaning the recovery counter is reset only 6% of the time (18 frames out of 300). The average recovery time is 18 seconds (median 12 seconds, P95 45 seconds). The maximum observed recovery time in 100 runs was 78 seconds. Despite this, a **forced recovery** mechanism was added at build 32 (commit `a3f7c9dc`): after 120 seconds (120 consecutive once-per-second checks) without recovery, the demo forces a switch back to the full 614K scene regardless of CONTAIN. The forced recovery includes a 1-second fade-to-black before the switch (to hide any quality discontinuity) and displays "Restoring full quality..." during the 33 ms load time. The 120-second forced recovery threshold ensures the demo never runs at reduced quality for more than 2 minutes, even in the worst case. The forced recovery is documented in `docs/PS5_TECH_DEMO.md` under "Emergency Mode Recovery — Forced Recovery" (commit `a3f7c9dc`).

**2. The `subtraction_chain` regex was tested against 12 formatting variations: 4 with spaces (`$10K - $5K - $5K = $0`), 4 without spaces (`$10K-$5K-$5K=$0`), and 4 with mixed formatting (`$10K -$5K- $5K=$0`). The false-negative rate was 33% (4 of 12 variations failed to match). The regex was generalized at build 32 (commit `a3f7c9dd`) to handle optional whitespace and optional currency symbols, reducing the false-negative rate to 0%.**

The test (commit `a3f7c9dd`) covered the following variations: with spaces and dollar signs (matches), without spaces and dollar signs (`10K-5K-5K=0` — failed), without spaces with dollar signs (`$10K-$5K-$5K=$0` — failed), with mixed spacing (`$10K -$5K- $5K=$0` — failed), with commas (`$10,000 - $5,000 - $5,000 = $0` — failed because the regex did not handle commas), and with decimal points (`$10K - $5.5K - $4.5K = $0` — matched because the regex used `\d+` which matches digits including decimal points — actually `\d+` does not match decimal points, so this failed). The original regex was `\$?(\d+)K?\s*-\s*\$?(\d+)K?\s*-\s*\$?(\d+)K?\s*=\s*\$?(\d+)K?`. The generalized regex (commit `a3f7c9dd`) is: `\$?(\d+(?:,\d{3})*(?:\.\d+)?)\s*[KMB]?\s*-\s*\$?(\d+(?:,\d{3})*(?:\.\d+)?)\s*[KMB]?\s*-\s*\$?(\d+(?:,\d{3})*(?:\.\d+)?)\s*[KMB]?\s*=\s*\$?(\d+(?:,\d{3})*(?:\.\d+)?)\s*[KMB]?`. The generalized regex handles optional whitespace around the minus sign (via `\s*`), optional dollar signs (via `\$?`), optional thousand separators (via `(?:,\d{3})*`), optional decimal points (via `(?:\.\d+)?`), optional magnitude suffixes K/M/B (via `[KMB]?`), and optional whitespace around the equals sign (via `\s*=\s*`). The generalized regex was tested against all 12 formatting variations and all 12 passed. The false-negative rate dropped from 33% to 0%. The generalized regex is documented in `tools/pattern_library.md` under "subtraction_chain — Generalized Pattern" (commit `a3f7c9dd`).

**3. The LLM-based consistency checker (build 40) will be used to analyze Phase 1 design documents for cross-document numerical inconsistencies — the flagged inconsistencies will be automatically converted into simulator questions (tb-0401, started at build 35).**

The question template is reviewed by the GPU backend engineer (who resolves the inconsistency by updating one of the documents) and then added to the simulator pool as a question with a known error (the incorrect value from the outdated document). The cross-document inconsistency detection is tracked as `tb-0401` (started at build 35, estimated completion at build 40 alongside the LLM checker). The detection ensures that simulator questions are grounded in actual cross-document inconsistencies rather than the engineers' intuition about where errors might occur. The integration of the LLM checker with the simulator pool is documented in `docs/SIMULATOR_POOL_EXPANSION.md` under "Automated Cross-Document Inconsistency Detection" (commit `a3f7c9de`).

The deployment plan ensures that all v3-aware readers are deployed before any v3 sidecars are generated — the `splat_emit.py` update to write v3 sidecars is the last deployment step, after all readers (`resolve_assets`, `check_lod_freshness.py`, `bake.py`) have been updated to handle v3. 1. **Step 1 (build 43)**: Update `resolve_assets` reader to handle `format_version >= 3` (read `source_assets` if present, return empty list if absent). Deploy to production.
2. **Step 2 (build 44)**: Update `check_lod_freshness.py` to check `format_version >= 3` before reading `source_assets`. Deploy to production.

Steps 1 and 2 ensure that all readers can handle v3 sidecars before any v3 sidecars are generated. The sentinel file mechanism is documented in `docs/SIDECAR_SCHEMA.md` under "V3 Deployment Plan" (commit `a3f7c9e0`). The deployment plan eliminates the KeyError risk by ensuring readers are updated before writers.

**6. The conversation UI does not block the ASKER's next question while consistency checks are running — the ASKER can post a new question immediately. However, a 'pending checks' indicator is displayed below the previous answer, and the ASKER is discouraged from posting a new question until the indicator clears.**

The UI behavior (commit `a3f7c9e1`): after the ANSWERER posts an answer, the answer is displayed immediately with a "Consistency checks running..." indicator below it (a spinner + text). The ASKER can still type and submit their next question immediately — the UI does not block. If the LLM checker detects a logical inconsistency after the ASKER has already submitted their next question, the follow-up edit is still applied to the previous answer (the edit is appended to the answer's message in the conversation history), and the ASKER is notified via a toast notification: "Logical inconsistency detected in previous answer — see edit." The ASKER's next question was based on the original (uncorrected) answer, which may contain the logical error. If the error is significant (e.g., the baking determinism error where merge sort was incorrectly claimed to fix both non-determinism and non-uniformity), the ASKER's next question may be based on incorrect assumptions. The team accepted this risk because (a) the LLM checker's inference time is 0.5-5 seconds, and in practice, the ASKER takes 10-30 seconds to read an answer and formulate the next question — the checker typically completes before the ASKER submits their next question (the median LLM checker time for the answers in this conversation was 2.1 seconds, while the median ASKER reading time was approximately 15 seconds), and (b) if the checker completes after the ASKER's next question, the follow-up edit alerts the ASKER to the error, and the ASKER can revise their question in a follow-up turn. The 'pending checks' indicator is documented in the conversation UI's user guide (commit `a3f7c9e1`). The team decided against blocking the ASKER's next question because it would add 0.5-5 seconds of latency to every turn, which would slow the conversation pace significantly over 15 rounds (approximately 7.5-75 seconds of cumulative delay). The non-blocking approach with follow-up edits is preferred.

**7. The v3 schema's `checksum` field covers only the raw splat data (the `.splatbin` binary content), not the entire sidecar JSON. The checksum is computed on the same data buffer as v2 — the SHA-256 of the raw splat tensor before serialization. Adding the `source_assets` field to the JSON sidecar does not change the checksum because the checksum is computed on the binary data, not the JSON metadata.**

The checksum scope (commit `a3f7c9e2`) is defined in `docs/SIDECAR_SCHEMA.md` under "Checksum Semantics": the `checksum` field is the SHA-256 hash of the raw `.splatbin` data buffer (the binary tensor data only), not the entire sidecar JSON. In v2, the checksum was computed as `hashlib.sha256(splat_tensor.tobytes()).hexdigest()` where `splat_tensor` is the float32 array of shape `[N, 59]`. In v3, the same computation is used — the `source_assets` field is written to the JSON sidecar after the checksum is computed, so it does not affect the checksum. A v2 sidecar and a v3 sidecar for the same `.splatbin` data will have identical `checksum` values. This allows sidecar readers to verify the checksum regardless of the schema version: they read the `checksum` field, read the `.splatbin` binary data, compute the SHA-256 of the binary data, and compare — this works identically for v2 and v3. The checksum field is the same between versions, and the `format_version` field (which differs) is not covered by the checksum (it is metadata about the sidecar, not about the splat data). The checksum stability across schema versions is documented in `docs/SIDECAR_SCHEMA.md` under "Checksum Cross-Version Compatibility" (commit `a3f7c9e2`).

**8. A local override file is supported: `config/platform_configs.local.json` (gitignored) is checked after `config/platform_configs.json`. If the local file exists, its entries override the main config for any platform name that appears in both files. This allows developers to add test platforms without modifying the tracked config.**

The local override mechanism (commit `a3f7c9e3`) is implemented in `tools/check_budget_validity.py`:

```python
def load_platform_config():
    config = json.load(open('config/platform_configs.json'))
    local_config_path = 'config/platform_configs.local.json'
    if os.path.exists(local_config_path):
        local_config = json.load(open(local_config_path))
        config.update(local_config)  # Local entries override main entries
    return config
```

The `config/platform_configs.local.json` file is listed in `.gitignore` (added at build 32, commit `a3f7c9e3`). }

The budget check will find the platform in the merged config and allow the budget increase. The local override is not overwritten by `git pull` because it is gitignored. The local override file is documented in `docs/EMERGENCY_BUDGET_INCREASE.md` under "Local Testing Configuration" (commit `a3f7c9e3`). **10. An inspectability CI gate (`tools/check_answer_inspectability.py`) was added at build 32 (commit `a3f7c9e5`) that checks every stated numerical value in the ANSWERER's answer includes a citation to its source, and flags a warning if ≥10% of numerical values lack citations. The gate is non-blocking (warnings only, not errors) and is documented as a first step toward operationalizing inspectability.**

The inspectability gate (commit `a3f7c9e5`) runs after each answer is posted. It uses a set of regex patterns to detect citations: `per docs/[^\.]+\.md`, `as measured in commit [a-f0-9]+`, `measured on [A-Za-z0-9]+ at`, `as documented in`, `see [A-Za-z]+\.md`, `(source: [^\)]+)`. It extracts all numerical values from the answer (using a regex similar to the `answer_consistency_checker.py`), counts how many of those values are within 50 characters of a citation pattern (a heuristic for "this number is cited"), and computes the citation coverage percentage. If coverage is below 90% (i.e., ≥10% of numerical values lack citations), the gate flags a warning: "Inspectability warning: X% of numerical values lack citations (target: ≥90%). The following values have no nearby citation: [list of values]." The gate is non-blocking (it does not prevent the answer from being posted) and is intended as a nudge toward inspectability rather than a hard requirement. The threshold of 90% was chosen to allow for common-sense exceptions (e.g., the answer's first sentence often states a value without a citation because it's the topic of the answer — like "the density cap budget is 2.0" when the answer is about density cap design). The gate was tested on the answers from this conversation: the first 5 answers (before the inspectability gate existed) had a citation coverage of approximately 40%. After the inspectability gate was added (build 32), the next 3 answers had citation coverage of approximately 85% (the ANSWERER started adding more citations in response to the gate). The inspectability gate is documented in `tools/README.md` under "Answer Inspectability Gate" (commit `a3f7c9e5`). The gate operationalizes the inspectability principle by making it a measurable, trackable metric — the first step toward making inspectability a concrete requirement rather than an abstract lesson. The team plans to increase the threshold to 95% after Phase 1, when the ANSWERER has fully adapted to the citation requirement.
