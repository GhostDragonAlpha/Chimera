RULE 1 DERIVATION - what question does lock_errors==0 answer?
-------------------------------------------------------------
The control plane's single-writer discipline is: every state transition runs
inside ONE SQLite BEGIN IMMEDIATE transaction (control.py call(), the
`con.execute('BEGIN IMMEDIATE')` line). Under that discipline a writer never
upgrades a deferred lock and every write conflict is a simple lock wait that
the busy handler retries; lock errors therefore require a writer to be stuck
behind contention for >busy_timeout. The assertion lock_errors==0 guards
REGRESSIONS OF THIS DISCIPLINE (someone replacing BEGIN IMMEDIATE with
BEGIN DEFERRED - the upgrade-deadlock class the busy handler cannot clear -
or serializing writes around client-visible waits). It does NOT guard host
contention delays: a lock error caused by the OS starving a correct writer
for 10 s is a different failure class, and at 8 in-process writers whose
client threads share a GIL with the service, starvation-driven lock errors
are routine on a loaded host. CONSEQUENCE (derived, not tasted): the
zero-lock claim is VALID exactly when writer-count x op-latency is far below
busy_timeout, and is NOISE exactly where the old test asserted it (8 writers,
20 s, in-process with the service). The assertion must move to the regime
where it discriminates, and the loaded regime must keep its measurement duty.
