# Common Rock Types & Their Formation Conditions — Reference

## Purpose
Reference card for rock classification by origin (igneous, metamorphic, sedimentary),
their physical properties, and formation conditions. Relevant to theMining, theDig,
and theScan membranes.

## Igneous Rocks (Crystallized from Magma/Lava)

### Classification (Mineral Composition)
| Series | Composition | Color | Typical Rock Names |
|--------|-------------|-------|---------------------|
| **Felsic** | >70% feldspar + quartz | Light (pink/white/gray) | Granite (intrusive), Rhyolite (extrusive) |
| **Intermediate** | 50–70% feldspar + amphibole/biotite | Medium | Diorite (intrusive), Andesite (extrusive) |
| **Mafic** | >70% pyroxene + olivine + Ca-plagioclase | Dark (black/green) | Gabbro (intrusive), Basalt (extrusive) |
| **Ultramafic** | >90% olivine + pyroxene | Dark, dense | Peridotite (intrusive), Komatiite (extrusive) |

### Physical Properties by Type

| Rock | Density (g/cm³) | Hardness (Mohs) | Color | Texture |
|------|-----------------|-----------------|-------|---------|
| Granite | 2.6–2.7 | 6–7 | Pink/white/gray | Coarse-grained, interlocking |
| Diorite | 2.8–3.0 | 6–7 | Gray/black | Medium-coarse, salt-and-pepper |
| Gabbro | 2.9–3.4 | 5–6 | Dark gray/black | Coarse, equigranular |
| Basalt | 2.8–3.0 | 5–6 | Black/dark gray | Fine-grained, glassy |
| Peridotite | 3.2–3.5 | 5–7 | Green/black | Coarse, granular (olivine visible) |
| Rhyolite | 2.3–2.5 | 6–7 | Light | Fine-grained, volcanic glass |
| Andesite | 2.5–2.7 | 5–6 | Gray/ purple | Fine-grained, porphyritic |
| Obsidian | 2.3–2.6 | 5–5.5 | Black, glassy | Glass (no crystals) |

### Formation Conditions

#### Intrusive (Plutonic) Igneous
| Rock | Crystallization Temp (°C) | Depth (km) | Cooling Rate |
|------|--------------------------|------------|--------------|
| Granite | 650–700 | 10–30 | Slow (years to millennia) |
| Diorite | 750–900 | 5–15 | Slow |
| Gabbro | 1000–1200 | 10–30 | Slow |
| Peridotite | 1200–1400 | 20–100 | Very slow |

#### Extrusive (Volcanic) Igneous
| Rock | Eruption Temp (°C) | Cooling Rate | Characteristics |
|------|---------------------|--------------|-----------------|
| Rhyolite | 700–900 | Instant to days | Explosive, glassy |
| Andesite | 900–1100 | Days to weeks | Viscous lava flows |
| Basalt | 1100–1250 | Minutes to days | Fluid flows, columnar joints |
| Obsidian | 1000–1100 | Hours | Volcanic glass |

## Weathering Products

### What Basalt Becomes (Laterization)
In tropical, wet climates with intense weathering:
```
Basalt → (weathering) →
  Clay minerals (kaolinite, smectite): 40–60%
  Iron oxides (Fe₂O₃·nH₂O): 20–40% (red/brown color)
  Alumina (gibbsite, corundum): 5–20%
  Residual: laterite or bauxite
```

### What Granite Becomes (Laterization)
```
Granite → (weathering) →
  Quartz (resistant): 40–60% (becomes sand)
  Clay minerals: 20–40%
  Feldspar → Kaolinite + dissolved ions
  Muscovite/ biotite → Chlorite + clay
  Residual: lateritic soil; quartz-dominated sand
```

### Desert Weathering (Basalt)
```
Basalt → (thermal fracturing + minimal chemical) →
  Volcanic breccia / scree
  Limited clay formation
  Iron oxidation without carbonation
  Produces: regolith with angular clasts
```

## Application to Mining Membranes

### Excavation Force Estimate
```
For a cutting tool with cross-sectional area A in rock:

F_cut = σ_c × A   (simplified; where σ_c = unconfined compressive strength)

Or more precisely:
F_cut = k₁ × σ_c × A_cut + k₂ × τ × A_cut

Where k₁, k₂ are geometry factors (~1–3 for typical tools).
```
