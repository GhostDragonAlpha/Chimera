Entering the Canyon

Sedimentary Zone — East Entrance
The canyon walls tell a story. Each layer is a chapter in the planet's geological history.

Sedimentary Sandstone

Geology
Compressed ancient dune beds. The cross-bedding shows wind direction shifted over centuries. This was once a desert.

Sedimentary Limestone

Geology
Marine fossil layer — calcium carbonate precipitate from countless tiny organisms. This was once a shallow sea.

Mid-Canyon Overview

Transition Zone
The terrain transitions from sedimentary to metamorphic as we walk deeper into the planet's geological past.

Metamorphic Schist

Geology
Heat and pressure altered the rock. The foliation bands record ancient mountain building — tectonic forces that shaped the planet.

Igneous Granite

Geology
Slow-cooled magma chamber. The large crystal grains visible to the naked eye indicate cooling over millions of years.

Igneous Basalt

Geology
Rapid-cooled volcanic flow. The columnar jointing is characteristic of slow, uniform cooling — this entire region was built by eruptions.

Reading the Clouds

Meteorology
Cumulus clouds with flat bottoms mean stable air. Cumulonimbus towers means seek shelter. The sky teaches weather prediction.

Navigating by the Stars

Astronomy
Stars shift position through the night as the planet rotates. Constellations look different from a different star system. You navigate by recognizing patterns.
